Why We Test

Questions We Could Not Ask Before

We have an idea. We know it is not completely wrong, because most of what it rests on is the Standard Model, among the most thoroughly tested theories in science. What we do not yet know is which of the new parts will hold. The path toward that answer will raise questions we could not have formulated without asking the first ones. That is why we test: not to win or lose, but to find out.

About the Author

Michael K. Baines

An aerospace engineer who began in refrigeration systems, moved into aircraft refrigeration and then into other aircraft systems. He worked on business jets for Dassault Falcon Jet, Gulfstream in Savannah, Georgia, and Bombardier's Global 6000 and 7000, taught engineering systems at Boeing, and was a flight test structural engineer at Mitsubishi on certification testing for what would have been its first in-house regional aircraft. Certification is where every claim about a design is stated in advance and then checked against test data. This book is tested the same way. He is an independent researcher based in Bangkok and founded the Ic² Research Institute.
