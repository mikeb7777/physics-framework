The Big TOE — Additional Resources

Go Deeper

The framework is an invitation, not a destination. These free courses, simulations, videos, and communities are for readers who want to engage directly with the physics behind the COSMIC Framework.

Difficulty level
Foundational No prior physics required | Intermediate Some calculus or physics helpful | Advanced University-level physics assumed
Colors after QCD: red, green, blue — the three color charges of quarks

All resources listed here are free to access. Links open in a new tab. The Ic² Research Institute has no affiliation with any of the platforms listed and receives no compensation for these recommendations. They are included solely because they are good.

▶ Video Lectures

PBS Space Time

Foundational

YouTube channel produced by PBS that covers quantum mechanics, black holes, information theory, cosmology, and spacetime with genuine scientific depth. The topics map directly onto the framework's territory and the production quality is exceptional.

Visit →

Crash Course Physics

Foundational

A fast-moving, engaging introduction to classical and modern physics. Covers mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, and quantum concepts in short episodes. Ideal starting point for readers with no prior physics background.

Visit →

The Theoretical Minimum — Leonard Susskind

Intermediate

Stanford's recorded lecture series by Leonard Susskind covering classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, special relativity, statistical mechanics, and cosmology. Rigorous but built for the motivated non-specialist. Susskind is one of the inventors of string theory and one of its clearest critics.

Visit →

3Blue1Brown

Intermediate

Visual mathematics at its best. The linear algebra, calculus, and neural network series provide the mathematical intuition underlying much of modern physics. If equations in the framework leave you wanting more foundation, start here.

Visit →

Perimeter Institute — PIRSA

Advanced

The Perimeter Institute Recorded Seminar Archive hosts thousands of lectures by leading researchers in quantum gravity, quantum foundations, cosmology, and quantum information. Graduate-level content from the researchers who are working on these questions now.

Visit →

MIT OpenCourseWare — Physics

Advanced

Full MIT physics courses with lecture notes, problem sets, and exams, at no cost. Covers classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, statistical physics, electromagnetism, and graduate-level special topics including quantum field theory and general relativity.

Visit →

⚙ Interactive Simulations

PhET Interactive Simulations

Foundational

Browser-based simulations from the University of Colorado Boulder covering quantum mechanics, wave behavior, atomic structure, nuclear physics, and more. No installation required. Exceptional for building physical intuition before engaging with formalism.

Visit →

Quantum Game

Intermediate

A browser-based puzzle game built on real quantum optics. Players route photons through polarizers, beam splitters, and detectors to complete levels, encountering genuine quantum behavior including superposition and interference along the way.

Visit →

IBM Quantum Composer

Intermediate

Build and run actual quantum circuits on IBM's real quantum hardware, free. The visual circuit composer requires no programming. A direct window into the quantum computing territory the framework discusses in Elements 11 through 13 and 21.

Visit →

Wolfram Physics Project Explorer

Advanced

Interactive tools for exploring the Wolfram Physics Project's hypergraph models of spacetime emergence. A different approach to the same fundamental problem the COSMIC Framework addresses: how does spacetime arise from something more primitive?

Visit →

📄 Written Resources

Khan Academy — Physics

Foundational

Structured, free courses in classical mechanics, thermodynamics, waves, electromagnetism, and quantum physics. Written lessons, worked examples, and practice problems at every stage. The clearest on-ramp for readers who want systematic coverage from the ground up.

Visit →

The Feynman Lectures on Physics

Intermediate

Richard Feynman's complete undergraduate physics lectures, free online in their entirety. Volume III covers quantum mechanics. Written with Feynman's characteristic refusal to accept an explanation he could not make clear. One of the best physics books ever produced.

Visit →

arXiv.org — Physics and Cosmology

Advanced

The preprint server where most physics research appears before journal publication. All five of the framework's registered predictions draw on papers published here. The gr-qc (general relativity and quantum cosmology) and hep-th (high-energy theory) sections are most relevant to the framework's questions.

Visit →

Zenodo — Open Research Repository

Advanced

The open-access repository where the COSMIC Framework's own preprints and data files are published. Also hosts preprints across all scientific disciplines. Peer-reviewed and grey literature both available, freely and permanently.

Visit →

◎ Communities and Discussion

r/AskPhysics — Reddit

Foundational

A large, moderated community where curious readers can ask physics questions and receive answers from students, researchers, and educators. Patient with genuine questions. A good place to test your understanding of concepts from the framework against people who will push back honestly.

Visit →

Physics Stack Exchange

Intermediate

A Q&A platform for physics questions with answers from researchers and advanced students. Answers are ranked and peer-reviewed by the community. More technical than Reddit; better for questions with precise, formal answers. The quantum mechanics and general relativity tags are particularly active.

Visit →

Ic² Research Institute Discussions

Intermediate

The institute's own discussion forum for questions about the COSMIC Framework, its predictions, and its implications. The right place to engage directly with the ideas in this book, challenge specific claims, and follow the progress of registered predictions as new data arrives.

Visit →

Perimeter Institute — Public Outreach

Advanced

The Perimeter Institute runs public lectures, workshops, and online learning programs bringing researchers in quantum gravity, quantum foundations, and cosmology into direct contact with engaged non-specialists. The International Summer School for Young Physicists and Perimeter Scholars International materials are freely available online.

Visit →

🔭 Observational Programs Referenced in the Framework

JWST — James Webb Space Telescope

Foundational

NASA's public portal for the James Webb Space Telescope, whose early galaxy observations are registered as a confirmed prediction of the framework (COSMIC-003). Image gallery, news, and plain-language explanations of results as they are released.

Visit →

DESI — Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument

Intermediate

DESI's dark energy evolution results are the basis for two registered framework predictions: COSMIC-001 and the black hole density prediction targeting DESI DR3. The public results portal releases survey data and summary papers as the five-year survey concludes.

Visit →

LIGO — Gravitational Wave Observatory

Intermediate

LIGO's public outreach portal covers gravitational wave detection, black hole mergers, and neutron star collisions. The gravitational wave signatures discussed in Element 19 come directly from LIGO and Virgo data. Event alerts and open data releases available.

Visit →

Euclid Mission — ESA

Advanced

The European Space Agency's Euclid mission is mapping the large-scale structure of the universe across ten billion years of cosmic history. Its results are a primary target dataset for the framework's dark energy mechanism investigation and the black hole density prediction.

Visit →
