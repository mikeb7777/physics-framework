# Version 6C: corrections, 30 September 2026 (conservation of information)

For Tina. Five sentences in 6C state the conservation of information more firmly than current physics supports. Checked against the field in September 2026: unitarity in closed quantum systems stands; the black hole information paradox is still formally unresolved, although since the 2019 Page-curve calculations most physicists expect information to be preserved. There is no experimental evidence either way for black holes.

Search for the quoted text in Word; each is unique.

## 1. Element 2: Landauer Principle Physical Information

**Current:**
> The total information content of the universe remains constant.

**Corrected:**
> Whether the universe as a whole behaves as one closed quantum system, so that its total information stays constant, is an open question.

**Why:** unitarity is established for closed systems; applying it to the whole universe (with expansion and horizons) is an assumption, not a result.

## 2. Element 19: Black Hole Information, The Ultimate Test

**Current:**
> The current weight of theoretical and experimental work supports the preservation of information. The question is not whether information is preserved, but how.

**Corrected:**
> The current weight of theoretical work supports the preservation of information; no experiment has yet tested it. For most physicists the question is no longer whether information is preserved, but how.

**Why:** there is no experimental work on black hole information preservation; the question is still formally open.

## 3. Element 19: Black Hole Information, The Ultimate Test

**Current:**
> The evidence now favors the information-first framework's prediction.

**Corrected:**
> The theoretical evidence now favors preservation, which the information-first framework requires, as does standard quantum mechanics.

**Why:** preservation is the expectation of standard quantum mechanics, shared by most approaches; under the evidence standard a shared expectation is not counted as this framework's prediction.

## 4. Element 20: Quantum Information Scrambling

**Current:**
> Quantum mechanics is unambiguous on this. Information is never destroyed, not even in a black hole.

**Corrected:**
> Standard quantum mechanics requires this, and the theoretical work of the last few years suggests that black holes obey it too.

**Why:** the sentence contradicted Element 19, which correctly calls the paradox "partially resolved."

## 5. Element 21: Quantum Error Correction

**Current:**
> Quantum systems conserve information: unitarity, the bedrock of quantum mechanics, forbids destruction.

**Corrected:**
> Closed quantum systems conserve information: unitary evolution, the bedrock of quantum mechanics, forbids its destruction.

**Why:** unitarity applies to closed systems; open systems exchange information with their surroundings, which is exactly what error correction fights.
