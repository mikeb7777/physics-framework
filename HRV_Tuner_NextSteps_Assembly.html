Next Steps & Prototype Assembly

Simple version — piezo contact mic, amp circuit, 3.5mm cable output
Phase 1 — Purchase
Source components and reference device
Bangkok local + Lazada. One week to gather everything.
1.1
Buy a reference medical stethoscope
Stemoscope (~฿1,500–2,500 on Lazada) or similar electronic stethoscope. Listen to it. Study the cup geometry and acoustic seal. This is your quality benchmark and your design reference in one purchase.
1.2
Buy a cheap donor stethoscope
Any basic medical stethoscope from a pharmacy (~฿100–200). You will disassemble the chest piece to study the cup dimensions and potentially use it as the housing for your first prototype.
1.3
Source electronic components
Ban Mo (Chinatown) or Pantip Plaza for: piezo discs (27mm), MAX4466 amp module, breadboard, jumper wires, 3.5mm audio jacks, CR2032 battery + holder, small slide switch, assorted resistors and capacitors. See build list for full spec.
1.4
Source tools if not already owned
Soldering iron, multimeter, wire stripper, helping hands clamp, hot glue gun. Most available at Pantip or any hardware store.
1.5
Pharmacy: gel and spare cups
Ultrasound gel (cleaner than vaseline, same effect) and a second cheap stethoscope for its cup. Also pick up medical-grade silicone sheet if available.
Phase 2 — Build
Breadboard prototype
One session, approximately 2–3 hours. No soldering yet.
Step by step

Breadboard Assembly Instructions

01
Prepare the piezo disc
The piezo disc has two terminals — the brass back plate (negative) and the silver ceramic face (positive). If leads are not already attached, solder a short wire to each terminal. Use the helping hands clamp to hold the disc steady. Keep heat brief on the ceramic face — piezos are heat sensitive.

Brass back = GND. Ceramic centre = signal out.
⚠ Do not bend the disc. Piezo ceramics are brittle. Hold flat at all times.
02
Place MAX4466 module on breadboard
The MAX4466 breakout module (common on AliExpress/Lazada) has 3 pins: VCC, GND, OUT. Place it near the centre of the breadboard. Connect:

VCC → positive rail
GND → negative rail
OUT → a free row (this is your amplified signal)
✓ The MAX4466 module has a small gain potentiometer (tiny screw). Leave it at midpoint for now — you will adjust it during testing.
03
Connect piezo to MAX4466 input
The MAX4466 breakout has an onboard MEMS mic which you bypass by connecting directly to its input pads — or more simply, connect the piezo signal wire to the same row as the module's input trace. Check your specific module's pinout — some expose an IN pad separately.

If no IN pad is accessible: connect piezo signal (ceramic) to OUT row via 10kΩ resistor, and piezo GND to the negative rail. This feeds the signal before amplification.
04
Add DC blocking capacitor on output
Connect a 10µF electrolytic capacitor between the OUT row and a new row — positive leg toward OUT, negative leg toward the new row. This new row is your audio signal output. The capacitor blocks any DC voltage from reaching the headphones, which protects both the headphones and your ears.
⚠ Electrolytic capacitors are polarised. Positive leg faces the signal. Reversed connection will damage the cap.
05
Connect 3.5mm audio jack
A standard 3.5mm stereo jack has three connections: Tip (left channel), Ring (right channel), Sleeve (ground).

Connect: audio signal row → Tip
Connect: audio signal row → Ring (same signal to both ears)
Connect: negative rail → Sleeve

Use a panel-mount jack with wire leads for breadboard testing, or alligator clip directly to a cut cable end.
06
Connect power — CR2032 + switch
CR2032 holder: positive → one terminal of slide switch → positive rail. Negative → negative rail directly. The switch breaks the positive supply line — simplest possible on/off.

Add a 100µF capacitor across the power rails (positive to positive rail, negative to negative rail) to filter any hum from the battery.
07
Prepare the cup
Take the chest piece from the donor stethoscope and remove the diaphragm. The remaining cup shell is your housing. Hot-glue the piezo disc flat against the inside face of the cup — ceramic face toward the skin side, brass back facing inward. Centre it as precisely as you can.

Run the piezo leads out through the tubing hole or a small notch in the rim.
✓ The stethoscope cup already has the correct geometry for a cardiac acoustic seal. This is why you buy the donor unit — free industrial design.
08
First test — signal check
Before headphones: plug a phone or recorder into the output jack and record while pressing the cup to your chest with a small amount of gel. Play back and check for S1/S2 heartbeat sounds. You should hear a clear "lub-dub" rhythm.

If signal is too quiet: turn MAX4466 gain potentiometer clockwise slightly. If you hear hum or noise: check capacitor polarity and power rail connections.
09
Headphone test — the real test
Plug standard wired headphones into the 3.5mm jack. Apply gel to the cup. Press to your chest at the left side, just below the nipple line, or slightly left of the sternum. Sit still and breathe normally.

You should hear your heartbeat clearly in both ears. Adjust gain as needed. The target: loud enough to count easily with eyes closed, quiet enough to be comfortable.
✓ Once you can count 5 beats and feel the urge to exhale naturally — the device is working correctly.
10
Record the optimal gain setting
Mark the potentiometer position with a pen. This setting becomes your production specification. All subsequent units are calibrated to this point before final assembly.
Phase 3 — Test
Validate the practice experience
Use it yourself first. Then two or three other people.
3.1
Personal practice sessions — minimum one week
Use it daily. Morning is optimal — resting heart rate is most stable and the practice anchors the day. Note any signal issues, cup seal problems, or cable management annoyances. These are product notes.
3.2
Compare against reference stethoscope
Use the Stemoscope side by side. Is the heartbeat sound comparable in clarity? Better or worse? This tells you whether your amp gain and cup design are adequate or need refinement before production.
3.3
Give it to two or three people with no instructions
Watch what they do. Where do they place the cup? What do they ask? What confuses them? This is your UX research and it directly shapes the insert card wording.
3.4
Photograph and document the working prototype
Circuit layout, cup assembly, final unit. These become your assembly reference for production units and are useful for any manufacturer conversation later.
Phase 4 — Launch
First listings and content
Only after prototype is validated and unit cost is confirmed.
4.1
Cost the production unit
BOM at small volume (10–20 units): piezo, amp module, cap, jack, battery, switch, cable, cup, gel sachet, extra cup, insert card, packaging. Target under ฿200 ($6) all-in. This determines final price and margin.
4.2
First AI video — the mom and daughter scene
Before any product listing goes live. This video is the context that makes the product legible. No voiceover. No sales language. The moment and the gesture do the work.
4.3
Product page on eequalsicsquared.com
Inside the Ic² context. The body literacy framework is already there. The HRV Tuner is the first physical instrument the Institute has produced — that framing is part of the product's value.
4.4
Etsy listing + one other marketplace
Etsy for wellness/independent audience. One additional platform — not Amazon. Each listing links to the practice videos. The listing text is minimal: what it is, what it does, what comes in the box.
4.5
Two or three Bangkok independent pharmacies
Approach in person with a working unit and a one-page leave-behind. Let them try it. If a pharmacist understands it they will recommend it. This is your local pilot and your first professional validation channel.
The minimum viable milestone One working unit that lets you count your own heartbeat clearly through standard headphones with eyes closed. Everything else follows from that. Don't move to Phase 3 until Phase 2 produces that result.
