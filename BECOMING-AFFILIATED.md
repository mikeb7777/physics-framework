# Becoming an affiliated institute

"Unaffiliated" is not a judgment about the work. It is an administrative fact
with concrete consequences: most grants need an institution to receive the
money, instrument time needs a team behind it, journals treat an unaffiliated
address as a weaker signal, and arXiv needs someone established to vouch for a
new author.

The useful question is which of those consequences can be lifted, and in what
order. The cheap steps first.

---

## Step 1. Get a ROR identifier (free, weeks)

The **Research Organization Registry** is the identifier system publishers,
Crossref, DataCite and Zenodo use to recognize an organization. A ROR ID is
what turns a name typed into an affiliation box into an organization a machine
can resolve.

The requirements are looser than most people assume. An organization **need not
be a legal entity**. It must be independent of any parent organization,
collective rather than one person, and enduring. It helps to be able to point
at research outputs that already carry the name.

- We already have outputs carrying the institute's name: the Zenodo deposits.
- The ask is a registry request at [ror.org](https://ror.org/).
- Effect: the institute's name resolves as an organization on every paper,
  dataset and deposit from then on.

**This is the highest-value item on the list and it costs nothing.**

## Step 2. ORCID, with the institute as employment (free, one day)

An ORCID record with the institute listed under Employment, linked to the ROR
ID once it exists. Every journal submission system reads ORCID, and the
affiliation travels with it automatically.

## Step 3. A Zenodo community (free, one day)

Zenodo supports curated communities. A community named for the institute
collects every pre-registration, dataset and paper under one heading with a
citable landing page, rather than leaving them as scattered deposits.

## Step 4. Decide the legal form (real decision, months, costs money)

This is the fork that everything expensive depends on.

| Route | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Stay informal | Nothing changes; ROR and ORCID still work | Nothing |
| Thai foundation or association | A legal person in the country of residence; can hold funds and sign agreements | Registration, a committee, minimum capital, annual filing |
| US nonprofit (e.g. 501(c)(3)) | Recognized by US funders and most publishers; can receive donations and some grants | Incorporation, filing fees, annual reporting, and it must be run properly |

**Do not start here.** Steps 1 to 3 give most of the recognition and none of
the ongoing obligation. A legal entity is worth it only when there is money to
receive or an agreement to sign, and it brings permanent paperwork.

## Step 5. Society membership (modest annual fee)

Membership of the American Physical Society or the Institute of Physics is open
to individuals without an institutional post. It does not confer affiliation,
but it provides meeting access, a member directory and a route to meeting
people, which matters for the next item.

## Step 6. The arXiv endorsement, which affiliation alone no longer solves

Worth stating precisely, because it changed recently. arXiv requires a new
author to be endorsed by an established author in the same subject area. As of
**21 January 2026**, arXiv no longer accepts an institutional email address as
the qualifier for endorsement, after a rise in non-scientific submissions.

So a ROR ID and a domain address will not open that door. The only route is a
person: someone actively publishing in the same area who is willing to read the
work and endorse it. The realistic path is to be cited-adjacent first, which
means producing something a specialist wants to check.

---

## What this changes on the site

Nothing yet, and that is the point: the Quest Map now says "Independent"
because that is the true description today. If and when steps 1 to 3 land, the
Independent lane gains a footnote rather than a new claim, and the record of
having been unaffiliated stays visible.

## Order of work

1. ROR registry request.
2. ORCID employment entry, linked to the ROR once issued.
3. Zenodo community.
4. Revisit the legal form only when there is money or an agreement in play.
5. Society membership when a meeting is worth attending.
6. Endorsement: a person, earned by the work, not bought by a domain.
