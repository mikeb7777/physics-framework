AI-Mediated Cognitive Extension

A Research Program of the Ic² Research Institute

The forty-bits-per-second channel capacity is a substrate constraint on biological cognition, not a ceiling on what cognition can do when paired with a complementary system. This program tests whether structured human-AI collaboration produces measurable gains in effective cognitive throughput by reorganizing what working memory carries rather than requiring it to expand.

The Core Premise

The constraint is real. The question is whether it is the limit it appears to be.

The Substrate Constraint

The human retina samples the visual field at approximately one billion bits per second. By the time that information reaches conscious awareness, it has been compressed by a factor of roughly 25 million to one. Forty bits per second reach experience. The rest is discarded, below the threshold, running processes whose outputs surface without explanation.

This is not a failure. The compression is doing essential work: filtering for survival-relevant signals, maintaining a coherent world-model, running autonomic and motor systems that would overwhelm conscious bandwidth if they required deliberate attention. The constraint is the system working as designed.

Working memory operates within that compressed channel. It holds approximately four to seven discrete items simultaneously. This limit is consistent across individuals regardless of intelligence, expertise, or education. An expert and a novice both face the same ceiling. What differs is not the ceiling but what occupies the space beneath it. Experts have converted frequently used knowledge into compressed patterns, a process cognitive science calls crystallized intelligence, and these patterns take up less working memory than the raw information did. The space is the same. The packing is more efficient.

The Composite Substrate Hypothesis

The hypothesis this program tests is not that AI augments human intelligence by being smarter. It is that a human-AI composite system reorganizes the cognitive work so that each component handles what it handles well, and the division produces effective throughput that neither achieves alone.

Biological cognition is adaptive, contextual, embodied, and capable of reasoning across domains with incomplete information. It also carries overhead: survival drives, emotional valence, social positioning, fatigue, and the compression artefacts of a system that evolved to prioritize threat detection over abstract reasoning.

AI carries none of that overhead. It handles pattern recognition, retrieval, compression, and parallel processing at scales biological working memory cannot sustain. It does not tire. It does not compete with itself for bandwidth. What it lacks is the adaptive contextual reasoning and the embodied understanding of consequences that makes biological cognition useful in the real world.

The composite system, in which biological adaptive reasoning operates on AI-compressed information, may produce outcomes that neither component could achieve by working alone. The program tests whether that is true, under what conditions, and with what measurable signatures.

The Crystallized Intelligence Problem

There is a well-documented paradox in expertise: as professionals accumulate domain knowledge, they convert adaptive problem-solving capacity (fluid intelligence) into stored pattern libraries (crystallized intelligence). Performance on familiar problems improves. Performance on novel problems degrades. The expert's working memory is increasingly occupied by the maintenance of what they already know, leaving less capacity for encountering what they don't.

This is documented in aviation, emergency medicine, and military command, all fields where crystallized expertise is essential but where novel situations routinely exceed the pattern library. The veteran sees clearly what resembles prior experience and misses what doesn't. The composite substrate hypothesis predicts that AI-assisted pattern matching frees working memory from pattern retrieval, allowing fluid intelligence to operate on the novel elements rather than being pre-empted by familiarity.

The underlying argument is developed in the "Speed of Novelty" article at eequalsicsquared.com/blog and in Element 17 of A Quest for the Big TOE.

The Bottleneck

The visual system samples roughly one billion bits per second. Forty reach conscious awareness. The 25-million-to-one compression runs entirely below the threshold of deliberate attention — and that is where almost all processing happens.

The Biological Ceiling

Cowan (2001) established this limit across extensive literature. Unlike fluid intelligence, the ceiling does not vary with expertise. The composite workflow hypothesis predicts throughput gains without changing this number — optimisation, not expansion.

Five Pre-Registered Predictions

All prediction sets are registered before data collection begins. All data is published open access. Null results are published in full.

01

Optimization, Not Expansion

Working memory capacity, measured directly, remains within normal biological limits throughout the program. Effective cognitive throughput, measured as the rate at which complex problems are resolved, increases. The program is testing reorganization of the cognitive load, not expansion of the biological channel. Any result showing measured working memory expansion would falsify the hypothesis.

Framework Prediction  Pre-Registered
02

Neuroplastic Adaptation Timeline

The transition from deliberate tool use to automatic cognitive extension follows a predictable neuroplastic timeline: three to twelve months for the composite workflow to shift from consciously managed to automatic. Participants will show distinct performance signatures at the pre-automatic and post-automatic phases, with the transition detectable through task performance data rather than self-report.

Testable  Pre-Registered
03

Autonomic Control Accelerates Adaptation

Participants with established autonomic regulation capacity, confirmed via heart rate variability measurement and biofeedback training, will show faster adaptation to the composite workflow than participants without it. Autonomic regulation is a proxy for the ability to consciously manage attentional bandwidth, which is the core skill the composite workflow requires.

Testable  Pre-Registered
04

Landauer Principle Heat Signature

Information erasure during cognitive processing produces heat at the theoretical minimum established by the Landauer principle (kT ln 2 per bit erased at room temperature). This prediction connects the cognitive extension research directly to the COSMIC Framework's claim that information processing is physical. It is the most technically demanding prediction and the one most directly tied to the underlying theory.

Framework Prediction
05

Fluid Intelligence Preservation Under Load

In cognitively loaded conditions including time pressure, novel problems, and high information density, participants using the composite workflow will maintain fluid intelligence performance (novel problem-solving) at levels significantly above a control group working without AI assistance. The prediction is specific: the preservation effect is larger for novel problems than for familiar ones, which distinguishes it from simple performance enhancement.

Testable  Pre-Registered

Null results are published with full methodology and completeness. This is a firm commitment, not a target. Results that falsify the predictions are published with the same completeness as results that confirm them.

Research Foundation

The program builds on established findings across cognitive science, neuroscience, and information physics.

Information Physics

The COSMIC Framework: Element 17

Element 17 of A Quest for the Big TOE establishes the mathematical basis for the 25-million-to-one compression ratio and frames it as a substrate constraint in information-theoretic terms rather than a purely biological limitation. The framework predicts that constraints on information processing are physical and should produce measurable physical signatures, which is the basis for Prediction 4 on Landauer heat signatures.

The COSMIC Framework treats consciousness as the last node in a long compression chain. The cognitive extension program tests whether that chain can be deliberately reorganized at the interface between biological and non-biological processing nodes.

Cognitive Science

Working Memory and Cognitive Load Theory

Cowan (2001) established the approximately four-item working memory limit across extensive experimental literature. Sweller's cognitive load theory provides the framework for understanding how different types of tasks compete for working memory resources. The distinction between intrinsic load (complexity inherent to the task), extraneous load (complexity added by poor design), and germane load (complexity that builds long-term schemas) maps directly onto the composite workflow design.

The program uses established cognitive load measurement protocols and extends them to composite human-AI workflows.

Neuroscience

Neuroplasticity and Tool Integration

Yue and Cole (1992) demonstrated 13.5% strength gain through mental imagery alone, establishing that the information processing target produces physical adaptation without physical exercise. This established the principle that neural reorganization follows informational patterns rather than purely physical training. The cognitive extension program applies this principle at the level of workflow integration: the prediction is that structured composite work produces neuroplastic changes in the brain regions responsible for cognitive control and working memory management.

Information Physics

Landauer's Principle

Landauer (1961) established that erasing one bit of information requires a minimum energy dissipation of kT ln 2 joules. This connects information processing irreversibly to thermodynamics, making information erasure a physical event with measurable physical consequences. Subsequent experimental confirmation (Bérut et al., 2012) established that the limit is measurable in real systems.

The cognitive extension program's fourth prediction, that information erasure during cognitive processing produces measurable heat signatures at the Landauer minimum, is designed to test whether biological cognition operates near the physical limit and whether AI-assisted processing shifts the heat signature in a predictable direction.

Program Structure

Phase One: Proof of Concept

Phase One establishes the measurement methodology, recruits and trains a participant cohort, and generates the first data against all five prediction sets. Duration: 18 months. The phase ends with a pre-registered analysis against the go/no-go criteria. Phase Two advancement requires demonstrated improvement in at least two of three primary cognitive metrics at statistically significant levels.

Phase One also develops and validates the composite workflow protocols that participants use. These protocols are themselves open-source outputs of the program, available to other researchers before Phase One concludes.

Budget range: $2–5M. The minimum budget enables proof-of-concept against the three behavioral prediction sets. The full budget adds the Landauer heat signature measurement and the autonomic control arm.

Phase Two: Validation and Scale

Phase Two applies validated protocols to a larger cohort, tests generalization across domains and participant profiles, and pursues the Landauer prediction with appropriate instrumentation if not completed in Phase One. Duration: 18 months from go/no-go decision.

Phase Two also develops the training materials and dissemination pathway for the composite workflow protocols. The objective is not a proprietary product but open methodology that any practitioner can apply.

Collaborators are sought for both phases: cognitive scientists for protocol design and analysis, neuroscientists for the neuroplasticity and autonomic components, and physicists with calorimetry experience for the Landauer measurement arm.

Open Science Commitments

All experimental protocols are pre-registered before data collection. All raw data is made publicly available upon publication. Analysis code is published under MIT license before results are released. Null results are published with full methodology. Equipment failures and setbacks are documented publicly with timestamps. The cognitive extension program operates under the same transparency principles as all Ic² Research Institute work, so the scientific record includes what did not work, not only what did.
